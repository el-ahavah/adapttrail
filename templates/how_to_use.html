Try your first adaptation journey.

You can explore examples without an account. Saving a project requires signing in.

  1. Explore a source

    Open Discover. Read the action, conditions, reported results and limitations. Check whether it is a documented external example, a self-reported community story or a fictional demo.

    Explore projects

  2. Create a private adaptation

    Select Adapt this project. Review the suggested action, add your own country and conditions, and describe what you would change and why. Sign-in returns you to the selected form.

  3. Plan with local guidance

    Suitability checks currently cover only three basic water-related approaches. If one is relevant, select it explicitly. You can optionally confirm a town for a saved weather snapshot. For other techniques, use the source information and seek local expertise. An assessment is not permission or proof that an action will work.

  4. Record observations

    Use Track progress for what you tried, difficulties and lessons. Measurements are optional; zero and missing data mean different things. Compare only compatible metrics, units and periods.

  5. Review your history

    Your history brings together saved assessments and observations. Current plans and status are shown separately; every edit is not recorded.

  6. Share only when ready

    Preview publication, review the public fields, and give consent. Private conditions, assessments and adaptation notes are not automatically shared. An ongoing project can be published with its uncertainties clearly stated. Unpublish removes public access but cannot retract copies already made.

Help us improve this pilot

After trying the journey, discuss these questions with the person helping you test AdaptTrail. This page does not collect or send feedback.

  • Could you tell which examples were real external accounts and which were demos?
  • Was it clear what stayed private and what publishing would reveal?
  • Were any terms, steps or weather notes confusing?
  • Did the source and assessment limits help you decide what needed local advice?
  • What was difficult on your phone or internet connection?
  • What would make you return to record another observation?

Use general observations; leave out passwords, exact addresses and private project details.
